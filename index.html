When you'd use it

Watching something that has no subtitles

  • Japanese, Korean or Chinese drama and anime raws, where the source is audio only
  • Foreign-language video on YouTube or in Safari with no subtitles in a language you read

Live streams — this kind of content can never have subtitles

  • Idol and creator live streams: nobody can subtitle them in advance
  • Sports commentary, keynotes, live shopping
  • A live stream happens as it happens. No subtitling group can prepare one ahead of time, which is what separates it from anything on demand.

Audio with no picture

  • Foreign-language podcasts, audiobooks, interviews — there is no video involved; it works on sound

Study and work

  • Online courses, lectures and talks in another language
  • Online meetings and briefings in another language

What it does: it turns the audio another app is playing into text, translates it, and floats the subtitles above whatever you are watching. You do not leave the app you are in, and you do not need a subtitle file.

What it does not do (these get mixed up a lot):

  • It is not a video editor that burns subtitles into videos you shot yourself
  • It does not download, store or export any video or audio
  • It does not translate text on web pages or in images — it works on sound
  • It does not work on a Mac: the iOS screen-sharing mechanism is not available on macOS

What you need

  • iPhone running iOS 26 or later
  • A network connection the first time you use a language, to download its model once. Fully offline afterwards.

How to put subtitles on video playing in another app

  1. Pick your languages. The two menus are the language being spoken and the language you want subtitles in.
  2. Tap the round button in the middle, "Start Sharing Audio". iOS shows its own Screen Sharing sheet — tap Start Sharing there. It is the only way an app can receive audio from other apps on iOS.
  3. Wait for the subtitle window to float up on its own, then switch to the browser or app playing your video. The subtitles keep updating in the floating window, which you can drag and resize.

You don't open the window yourself: the app opens it for you once the audio share is actually running. The window has nothing to show until audio is reaching it, which is why that order is fixed.

If the system closes the window — iOS allows only one Picture in Picture at a time, so another app starting its own takes yours over — the audio share carries on, and the app's button becomes Open Subtitle Window. One tap brings it back.

Which of the three subtitle styles to use

Under the language menus is Subtitle Style: Short, Long and Slim, drawn as the shapes of the three floating windows. Long, in the middle, is the default: it shows the original sentence together with its translation, while the other two show the translation only. Once you change it, the app remembers your choice. The style can't be changed while the window is open — that row is dimmed, so close the window first.

How the free sessions work, and how to remove the limit

  • A new install gets 3 free captioning sessions, and one more every week after those run out.
  • A session only counts once captions actually appear — opening the window by mistake and closing it costs nothing.
  • A session is never cut short. Watch for as long as you like; it stays one session.
  • To remove the limit, the app offers a monthly subscription, a yearly subscription, or a one-time lifetime unlock.

New phone, or reinstalled the app — restoring what you bought

Tap the gear in the top right of the app to open Settings, then tap Restore Purchases, signed in with the same Apple Account you bought with.

Cancelling a subscription

Subscriptions are managed by Apple, not inside this app: open Settings → tap your name at the top → Subscriptions, then select Realtime Subtitles and cancel. The lifetime unlock is a one-time purchase, never renews, and needs no cancelling.

No subtitles appearing — how to check

  • Check the line of status text above the big button on the app's main screen — it names whichever stage is pending (model downloading, language pack not installed, audio not being shared). No line there means there is nothing for you to do.
  • Make sure the video is not muted and the volume is up.
  • Look for the red recording indicator in the status bar, which means audio really is being shared.
  • Changing the spoken language requires starting audio sharing again. The subtitle language can be changed at any time.
  • Recognition works from sound, so heavy background music or several people talking at once will noticeably reduce accuracy.
  • Some language combinations aren't available on a given device; the app will tell you and ask you to pick another.
